Travel · getting here

First, get yourself to Veneto.

There are several airports that work well. Which one is best mostly depends on your flight price, your plans before and after the wedding, and whether you want to rent a car.

Our personal advice: if you want to explore the hills or the Dolomites, rent a car. If you are only coming for the wedding, public transport can work — and if you need help getting to the venue, please tell us. We can organise lifts.
TSF
Treviso
A very practical option for Padova. There are public bus connections towards Padova and the Euganean area.
VRN
Verona
AirLink takes you to Verona Porta Nuova; from there, trains make the rest of the journey straightforward.
BLQ
Bologna
Often worth checking for good flights. The airport-city transfer is convenient, although surprisingly pricey for such a short hop.
VCE
Venice
Easy to combine with Venice or Mestre, then continue towards Padova by train or coach.
BGY
Bergamo
Farther away, but absolutely worth considering if the flight saving is big enough. Check long-distance coaches for the onward journey.
Moving around Italy
By train
Trenitalia runs both regional trains and faster long-distance services. Italo is another excellent high-speed option and can be particularly good value when booked ahead.
By bus
FlixBus and Itabus are worth checking for longer journeys, particularly when flying into a more distant airport.
From Treviso
MOM and Busitalia operate connections towards Padova. You can also take the local airport bus to Treviso railway station and continue by train.
To Teolo
Public buses exist, but the timetable is less flexible than in the cities. For exploring the area, a car is much easier.
Wedding day
No car? Please tell us.
Do not rent a car just for the wedding if you do not need one. We can try to organise lifts with other guests. Just write to us in advance so nobody has to spend the evening studying rural bus timetables.
Travel · while you're here

Make a little holiday of it.

These are not a definitive guide to northern Italy. They are simply the places — and the food — we would genuinely tell our friends not to miss.
01 · day trip

Venice

Take the train from Padova, arrive early and let yourself get lost. Walk away from the busiest streets, stop for cicchetti, and stay until the light starts to change. Before going, check the official Venice website to see whether the 2027 Access Fee applies to your date.

train friendly
02 · if you have a car

Dolomiti

If you have a little extra time, go. The Dolomites are worth the detour. Leave very early for a long day or, even better, spend a night in the mountains.

best with a car
03 · classic

Verona

See the Arena di Verona — the Roman amphitheatre — do the shamelessly touristy Juliet stop, wander the old centre, then head up to Castel San Pietro for the view.

easy by train
04 · eat first

Bologna

Walk under the porticoes, hunt down the Seven Secrets of Bologna, and make time for proper ragù. Our ritual is tagliatelle at Osteria dell'Orsa.

pasta mandatory
05 · right here

Colli Euganei

Do not rush away from the hills. Drive slowly, stop at viewpoints, visit Arquà Petrarca, taste local wine, or disappear into the thermal spas around Abano and Montegrotto for an afternoon.

slow travel
06 · closest city

Padova

Prato della Valle, the old centre, Sant'Antonio, an evening aperitivo and a proper spritz. It is also one of the easiest places to stay if you are travelling without a car.

spritz territory
07 · a little further

Firenze

If you have an extra day, take the high-speed train and make a day of Firenze: see the Duomo and Ponte Vecchio, wander through the historic centre, and finish at Piazzale Michelangelo for the view. If you can, stay for dinner before heading back.

worth the train

Eat &
drink well.

Spritz in Padova

Spritz is pleasantly affordable around Padova. Try the classics: Aperol Spritz for everyone, Campari Spritz for the boys and Hugo Spritz for the girls.

Pizza

La Mafaldina is a Padova favourite. Rossopomodoro is another easy option and is useful when someone in the group needs gluten-free choices — check the branch when booking.

Pasta & Others

Non-negotiable. In Bologna, Osteria dell'Orsa is THE ragù stop. In Venice, add cicchetti. In the hills, look out for local wine and whatever is seasonal.

Where should you base yourself?

Teolo & the Colli Euganei

Best if you want countryside, quiet mornings and to be close to the wedding. A car makes this option much easier.

Abano / Montegrotto

A good middle ground: plenty of hotels and spas, close to the hills, and more practical than staying somewhere very rural.

Padova

Our easiest choice without a car. Great train connections for Venice, Verona and Bologna, plus restaurants, bars and aperitivo on your doorstep.
